The climate change effect on Pantanal

By Gabriel Croquer

The latest biome suffering from extreme weather events due to the climate crisis is Pantanal, the world’s biggest tropical wetlands, which territory got warmer and drier in the last 60 years.

The territory

Even though it is considered the biome with the smallest territorial extension in Brazil, the Pantanal spreads trough a continental area of ​​150,355 km². In Brazil, it is located in the territories of Mato Grosso and Mato Grosso do Sul.

Climate change

This is another year of burnings starting earlier than expected. The amount of territory burnt is getting close to the level of 2020’s catastrophe, the worst in history.

To understand how this could’ve been impacted by global warming, I analyzed data collected in a study which mapped climate parameters every day since 1950 in all brazilian cities. Let’s take a closer look at how this world changed for Pantanal.

Climate change

As you can see in the map above, all cities in the Pantanal territory have become hotter in the last 60 years. The higher temperature helps keep the region dry, facilitating new fires in the biome.

There is some cities in which the mean temperature rose up to 0,60°C.

The data analyzed also shows that the rainfall volume plummeted in all cities in the biome.

The rise of temperature is happening globally, due to human actions on enviroment. Pantanal is just of one of the various biomes that will see its natural phenomenons turn into catastrophes.
